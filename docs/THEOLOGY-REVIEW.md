# Bible Connections: content review packet

Prepared for pastoral and theological review. Thank you for giving your time to this.

## 1. What Bible Connections is

[Bible Connections](https://joemighty.github.io/BibleConnections/) is a free website that maps about 200,000 cross references in the Bible as arcs from Genesis to Revelation. People can tap any arc to see two passages that point to each other, then read them on Bible.com in the translation they choose.

The site does not publish any Bible translation. Every passage opens on Bible.com. The only Scripture text shown on the site is the original Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek, from STEP Bible (Tyndale House, CC BY 4.0).

Some parts of the site are written by the project, not taken from a source. These parts shape how people read the Bible, so we want a pastor to check them before more are added.

## 3. Content principles

These guide everything the project writes.

1. **Scripture first.** Notes explain why passages connect. They do not add teaching that the passages do not support.
2. **Christ-centred.** The Old and New Testaments are read as one story that finds its fulfilment in Jesus, following how the New Testament writers read the Old Testament.
3. **Honest about the kind of link.** A direct quotation, a "this was to fulfil" statement, a pattern (typology) and a faint echo are labelled differently, so readers do not treat a possible echo as a certain prophecy.
4. **Charitable where Christians differ.** Where faithful Christians hold different views, the site says so briefly and does not take a side unless the church asks it to.
5. **Plain language.** Written for someone opening the Bible for the first time, at about a 12 year old reading level. A kids mode uses simpler words.
6. **No copied commentary.** All explanations are written fresh. Public domain reference works (for example Easton's Bible Dictionary) may be used for research, never copied.
7. **No translation text.** Translations are protected by copyright, so the site links to Bible.com and never reproduces their wording.

**Questions for you:** Is anything missing? Should the site state a particular doctrinal position anywhere?

## 4. Reflection questions (live on the site)

Every verse page ends with three questions. They depend on the kind of book the verse is in. Each set moves from noticing, to understanding, to responding.

**Law**

1. What does this passage show about who God is and what he values?
2. How does this instruction or story fit the bigger story of God rescuing his people?
3. Where does this point ahead to Jesus, and what does that mean for how you live this week?

**History**

1. Who are the people in this passage, and what choices do they make?
2. Where do you see God at work, even quietly, in what happens?
3. What does this story teach you about trusting God in your own circumstances?

**Wisdom and Poetry**

1. What feelings or experiences are expressed here, and do any feel familiar?
2. What does this passage say about God's character or his ways?
3. How could you pray these words, or live out this wisdom, today?

**Major Prophets**

1. What was happening to God's people when this message was first given?
2. What warning or promise does God give here, and how does the New Testament take it up?
3. Where do you need to hear this warning or hold on to this promise?

**Minor Prophets**

1. What problem among God's people is the prophet speaking to?
2. What does this passage reveal about God's justice and his mercy?
3. What would it look like to respond to this message in your life and community?

**Gospels**

1. What does Jesus say or do here, and how do the people around him respond?
2. What does this show you about who Jesus is?
3. If you had been there, how would you have responded, and how will you respond now?

**Acts**

1. How is the Holy Spirit at work in this passage?
2. What helps or hinders the good news as it spreads here?
3. Where might God be sending you to share or live out the good news?

**Paul's Letters**

1. What is Paul's main point here, and why did this church need to hear it?
2. What does this passage say God has done for us in Christ?
3. What is one way this truth should change how you think, speak or act?

**General Letters**

1. What challenge or encouragement is the writer giving his readers?
2. How does this passage connect faith with everyday life?
3. What is one practical step you could take in response?

**Revelation**

1. What images stand out to you here, and what might they have meant to the first readers?
2. What does this passage show about Jesus and his victory?
3. How does this hope help you stay faithful today?

## 5. Book introductions (live on the site)

Each verse page shows a short introduction to its book. Where authorship is debated, the wording uses "traditionally linked to" or describes the setting the book gives.

### Law

- **Genesis:** The book of beginnings: creation, the fall, the flood and God's promise to Abraham's family. Traditionally linked to Moses, it sets up the whole story of God's plan to bless all nations.
- **Exodus:** God rescues Israel from slavery in Egypt, gives the law at Mount Sinai and comes to live among his people in the tabernacle. The Passover and the exodus shape the rest of Scripture's picture of salvation.
- **Leviticus:** Instructions for worship, sacrifice and holy living given at Sinai. It shows how a holy God can live among an imperfect people, and its sacrifices form the background to the New Testament's teaching on Jesus.
- **Numbers:** Israel's forty years in the wilderness between Sinai and the promised land. A story of complaint and unbelief, and of God's patience and faithfulness to a new generation.
- **Deuteronomy:** Moses' farewell speeches on the edge of the promised land, calling Israel to love the Lord with all their heart and to remember his faithfulness. Jesus quotes it more than almost any other book.
### History

- **Joshua:** Israel enters and settles the promised land under Joshua. It celebrates God keeping his promises and ends with a call to choose whom the people will serve.
- **Judges:** A repeating cycle of turning away, oppression, crying out and rescue through leaders called judges. It shows how quickly a people drift when everyone does what is right in their own eyes.
- **Ruth:** A short story of loyalty and kindness set in the time of the judges. A Moabite widow becomes part of Israel and an ancestor of King David, and later of Jesus.
- **1 Samuel:** Israel moves from judges to kings: Samuel the prophet, Saul the first king, and the rise of David. It contrasts outward appearance with a heart that trusts God.
- **2 Samuel:** The reign of King David, including God's covenant promise of a lasting throne and the painful results of David's sin. The promise to David becomes central to the hope for the Messiah.
- **1 Kings:** Solomon's wisdom and temple, then the kingdom splits in two. Prophets like Elijah call kings and people back to the Lord.
- **2 Kings:** The decline of Israel and Judah, ending with exile to Assyria and Babylon. It explains the exile while keeping hope alive through God's promise to David.
- **1 Chronicles:** A retelling of Israel's history for those returning from exile, beginning with family lists and focusing on David and preparations for worship in the temple.
- **2 Chronicles:** The story of the kings of Judah from Solomon to the exile, highlighting times of renewal when people humbled themselves and sought God. It ends with a decree allowing the return home.
- **Ezra:** Exiles return to Jerusalem, rebuild the temple and recommit themselves to God's law under Ezra the scribe.
- **Nehemiah:** Nehemiah leads the rebuilding of Jerusalem's walls despite opposition, and the people renew their covenant with God. A model of prayer joined with practical action.
- **Esther:** A Jewish woman in the Persian court risks her life to save her people. God is never named, yet his care is seen in every turn of the story.
### Wisdom and Poetry

- **Job:** A righteous man suffers terrible loss, and he and his friends wrestle with why. It refuses simple answers and ends with God speaking from the whirlwind.
- **Psalms:** Israel's songbook of 150 prayers and songs: praise, lament, thanksgiving, confession and trust. Many are linked to David, and the New Testament often reads them as pointing to Jesus.
- **Proverbs:** Short sayings on living wisely in everyday life: work, words, money, friendship and family. It begins with the fear of the Lord as the start of knowledge.
- **Ecclesiastes:** An honest look at life's frustrations under the sun. The Teacher finds meaning not in achievement or pleasure but in enjoying God's gifts and fearing him.
- **Song of Songs:** A poem celebrating love between a bride and groom. It honours married love as a good gift, and many readers have also seen in it a picture of God's love for his people.
### Major Prophets

- **Isaiah:** Isaiah calls Judah to trust God and warns of judgement, yet speaks some of Scripture's richest promises: a child called Immanuel, a suffering servant and a new creation. The New Testament quotes it often.
- **Jeremiah:** Jeremiah warns Jerusalem before its fall to Babylon and weeps over its people. In the darkness he announces a new covenant written on the heart.
- **Lamentations:** Five poems of grief after Jerusalem's destruction. At its centre is a confession that the Lord's mercies are new every morning.
- **Ezekiel:** A priest among the exiles in Babylon sees visions of God's glory leaving and one day returning. He promises a new heart, a new spirit and dry bones brought to life.
- **Daniel:** Stories of faithfulness in a foreign empire and visions of kingdoms rising and falling. Parts are written in Aramaic. It looks to an everlasting kingdom given to one like a son of man.
### Minor Prophets

- **Hosea:** Hosea's painful marriage becomes a picture of God's faithful love for unfaithful Israel, and of his longing to bring his people back.
- **Joel:** A locust plague becomes a call to return to God. Joel promises that God will pour out his Spirit on all people, a promise Peter quotes at Pentecost.
- **Amos:** A shepherd sent to the prosperous northern kingdom confronts injustice and empty religion, calling for justice to roll on like a river.
- **Obadiah:** The shortest book of the Old Testament announces judgement on Edom for turning on its brother Israel, and looks to the day when the kingdom will be the Lord's.
- **Jonah:** A reluctant prophet runs from God, is swallowed by a great fish and sees an enemy city repent. It shows God's compassion reaching beyond Israel.
- **Micah:** Micah condemns corrupt leaders, foretells a ruler born in Bethlehem and sums up what God asks: to act justly, love mercy and walk humbly with him.
- **Nahum:** A poem announcing the fall of Nineveh, the capital of cruel Assyria. It comforts the oppressed with the truth that God is good and a refuge.
- **Habakkuk:** A prophet questions God about injustice and learns to wait in faith. It gives the line the righteous shall live by faith, which Paul takes up.
- **Zephaniah:** A warning of the coming day of the Lord, followed by a tender promise that God will rejoice over his people with singing.
- **Haggai:** After the exile, Haggai urges the returned people to stop neglecting God's house and rebuild the temple, promising a greater glory to come.
- **Zechariah:** Visions encouraging the rebuilding of Jerusalem and pointing ahead to a humble king riding on a donkey and a pierced one who is mourned. The Gospels draw on it at Jesus' final week.
- **Malachi:** The last Old Testament prophet challenges careless worship and promises a messenger who will prepare the way for the Lord, linked in the Gospels to John the Baptist.
### Gospels

- **Matthew:** The Gospel written with Jewish readers in mind, showing Jesus as the promised King who fulfils the Scriptures. It includes the Sermon on the Mount and ends with the Great Commission.
- **Mark:** The shortest and fastest moving Gospel, often linked to Peter's preaching. It shows Jesus as the Son of God who came to serve and give his life as a ransom.
- **Luke:** A careful account written for Theophilus, with special attention to outsiders, the poor, women and prayer. Luke continues the story in Acts.
- **John:** Written so that readers may believe Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and have life in his name. It is built around signs and the I am sayings.
### Acts

- **Acts:** The risen Jesus sends his Spirit, and the good news spreads from Jerusalem to Rome through Peter, Paul and many others.
### Paul's Letters

- **Romans:** Paul's fullest explanation of the gospel: all have sinned, and God puts people right with himself through faith in Jesus. It then shows how grace shapes everyday life.
- **1 Corinthians:** Paul writes to a gifted but divided church about unity, the cross, spiritual gifts and the resurrection. It includes the well known chapter on love.
- **2 Corinthians:** A personal letter in which Paul defends his ministry and shows that God's power is made perfect in weakness.
- **Galatians:** A passionate defence of the gospel of grace: people are made right with God through faith in Christ, not by keeping the law, and are free to walk by the Spirit.
- **Ephesians:** Paul celebrates every spiritual blessing in Christ and the one new people God has made from Jews and Gentiles, then describes life together in the church and the home.
- **Philippians:** A warm letter written from prison, full of joy. It includes a hymn about Christ humbling himself and being exalted.
- **Colossians:** Paul shows the supremacy of Christ over all creation and urges believers not to be drawn away from him.
- **1 Thessalonians:** One of Paul's earliest letters, encouraging a young church facing opposition and giving hope about Jesus' return.
- **2 Thessalonians:** Paul corrects confusion about the day of the Lord and encourages steady faith and honest work while waiting.
- **1 Timothy:** Guidance for Timothy on leading the church in Ephesus: sound teaching, prayer, leadership and godly living.
- **2 Timothy:** Paul's last letter, written near the end of his life, urging Timothy to stay faithful, guard the gospel and keep preaching the word.
- **Titus:** Instructions for Titus as he organises churches on Crete, showing how God's grace trains people for good works.
- **Philemon:** A short personal letter asking Philemon to welcome back Onesimus, a runaway slave, as a brother in Christ.
### General Letters

- **Hebrews:** Written to believers tempted to turn back, it shows Jesus as greater than angels, Moses and the priesthood, and calls readers to persevere in faith.
- **James:** Practical wisdom from James, the brother of Jesus: true faith shows itself in action, careful speech and care for the poor.
- **1 Peter:** Peter encourages Christians facing suffering, reminding them of their living hope and their calling as God's people.
- **2 Peter:** A final reminder from Peter to grow in grace, hold to the truth against false teachers and look forward to the Lord's return.
- **1 John:** A letter of assurance: God is light and God is love, and those who know him walk in the light and love one another.
- **2 John:** A brief letter urging believers to keep loving one another and to hold to the truth about Jesus.
- **3 John:** A short personal note praising Gaius for his hospitality to travelling teachers.
- **Jude:** A brief, urgent call to contend for the faith against those who twist grace, ending with a well known blessing.
### Revelation

- **Revelation:** A vision given to John, full of symbols, showing the risen Christ, the struggle between God's kingdom and evil, and the hope of a new heaven and new earth. Christians read its details in different ways, but agree on its message that Jesus wins.


## 6. Draft prophecy and fulfilment chains (not on the site yet)

Each chain will appear as connected stages, light up its arcs on the map, and link to Bible.com for every passage. The **type** tells readers how strong the link is:

- **Quotation:** the New Testament quotes the Old Testament passage.
- **Fulfilment:** the New Testament says the event happened "to fulfil" the passage.
- **Pattern:** a person, event or institution that the New Testament treats as foreshadowing Christ (typology).
- **Promise:** a covenant promise that later passages say is kept.

| # | Chain | Type | Draft explanation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Isaiah 52:13 to 53:12, then Acts 8:32 to 35, then 1 Peter 2:22 to 25 | Quotation | Isaiah describes a servant who suffers silently and carries the sins of others. When an Ethiopian official reads this passage, Philip starts there and tells him about Jesus. Peter later uses the same chapter to explain the cross and call believers to follow Christ's example. |
| 2 | Genesis 12:1 to 3, then Galatians 3:8, then Revelation 7:9 | Promise | God promises Abraham that all nations will be blessed through him. Paul calls this promise the gospel announced in advance. Revelation shows its completion: a crowd from every nation worshipping together. |
| 3 | 2 Samuel 7:12 to 16, then Luke 1:32 to 33, then Acts 2:29 to 36 | Promise | God promises David a descendant whose kingdom will last for ever. The angel tells Mary that her son will receive David's throne. At Pentecost Peter says the resurrection shows that Jesus is this promised king. |
| 4 | Micah 5:2, then Matthew 2:4 to 6 | Quotation | Micah names Bethlehem as the birthplace of a ruler for Israel. When Herod asks where the Messiah will be born, the scholars answer from this passage. |
| 5 | Isaiah 7:14, then Matthew 1:22 to 23 | Fulfilment | Isaiah gives King Ahaz a sign: a child called Immanuel, "God with us". Matthew sees the full meaning of the sign in the birth of Jesus. *Note for reviewer: many scholars see a first fulfilment in Isaiah's own time as well. See section 7.* |
| 6 | Psalm 22, then Matthew 27:35 to 46, then John 19:23 to 24 | Quotation | David's psalm moves from feeling abandoned by God to praise. On the cross Jesus prays its opening line, and the Gospel writers notice details of the psalm in the crucifixion, such as the dividing of his clothes. |
| 7 | Exodus 12, then John 1:29, then 1 Corinthians 5:7 | Pattern | At the first Passover a lamb's blood protects Israel from judgement. John the Baptist calls Jesus the Lamb of God, and Paul calls Christ our Passover lamb. |
| 8 | Jeremiah 31:31 to 34, then Luke 22:20, then Hebrews 8:6 to 13 | Promise | Jeremiah promises a new covenant with the law written on hearts and sins forgiven. At the Last Supper Jesus calls the cup the new covenant in his blood. Hebrews explains that this covenant is better than the old. |
| 9 | Zechariah 9:9, then Matthew 21:1 to 5, then John 12:14 to 16 | Quotation | Zechariah pictures Jerusalem's king arriving humbly on a donkey. Jesus enters Jerusalem this way, and John notes that the disciples only understood the connection later. |
| 10 | Joel 2:28 to 32, then Acts 2:14 to 21 | Quotation | Joel promises that God will pour out his Spirit on all kinds of people. At Pentecost Peter says, "this is what was spoken by the prophet Joel". |
| 11 | Daniel 7:13 to 14, then Mark 14:61 to 62, then Revelation 1:7 | Quotation | Daniel sees one like a son of man receive an everlasting kingdom. At his trial Jesus applies this vision to himself, and Revelation pictures his return in the same terms. |
| 12 | Genesis 3:15, then Romans 16:20, then Revelation 12:9 | Promise | After the fall God says the woman's offspring will crush the serpent. Paul and John use this picture of Satan defeated. *Note for reviewer: many Christians read Genesis 3:15 as the first gospel promise, while others read it more generally. See section 7.* |

## 7. Where Christians disagree: how should the site handle these?

For each topic the draft approach is a short neutral note. Please choose: **keep the neutral note**, **state the church's view** (please give it), or **leave the topic out**.

| Topic | Draft neutral note |
| --- | --- |
| Isaiah 7:14 | "Many readers see a sign for King Ahaz in Isaiah's day, which Matthew shows reaches its full meaning in the virgin birth of Jesus." |
| Genesis 3:15 | "Many Christians, from the early church onward, have read this as the first promise of the gospel." |
| Revelation and the end times | "Christians read the details of Revelation in different ways, but agree that Jesus will return and make all things new." |
| Israel and the church | "The New Testament shows Gentiles brought into God's people through Jesus. Christians differ on what this means for the future of Israel." |
| Dating of Daniel and Isaiah | The site does not discuss dating debates. It describes each book in the setting the text itself gives. |
| Song of Songs | "It honours married love as a good gift, and many readers also see in it a picture of God's love for his people." (already on the site) |
| Spiritual gifts | Not discussed beyond describing what passages say. |
